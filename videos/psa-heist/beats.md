# PSA truck heist: beat sheet (stage 1)

62.8 s · 1884 frames at 30 fps · the voiceover is fixed.

## Logline
A thief smashes the lock on a FedEx truck and drives off with a million dollars of graded cards, only to find that the thing guarding them was never the lock. It was a number printed on every label.

## The problem with this voiceover, and the fix
The script opens like a news wire: date, city, truck (7.4 s before anything happens). The new rule says the first second must be an action.

**The fix is a rewind hook.** Frame 0 is the hammer at the top of its swing over the padlock. It strikes on "13", the lock bursts, and everything freezes mid-air while the title "NGÀY 13 / THÁNG 10" slides in. Then the whole moment rewinds: the debris flies back onto the hasp and the camera flies backwards up into the sky over Santa Ana. The voice is now "setting the scene", and the viewer already knows where it's going. The same strike plays for real at 9.7 s and finishes.

The rewind reuses the lock physics we already baked (played backwards), so it costs little.

The ending flies back along the slab's journey and lands on the frame-0 picture, so the reel **loops**.

## Spine (cause and effect)
1. A truck full of freshly graded cards is left alone.
2. The lock is smashed and the pallets are taken.
3. The loss is counted (6,451 items, ~3,000 Pokémon, ~$1M) and the police and FBI arrive.
4. **The turn:** the real protection is the cert number in PSA's database.
5. PSA voids every number, so the slabs turn red everywhere.
6. Selling exposes the thief; cracking the case destroys the grade.
7. The lesson: check the cert, because the record is what makes the card.

## Hero objects
- **The brass padlock:** smashed, rewound, smashed for real, bagged by the police, and the keyhole we fly through at the turn.
- **The Charizard slab (slab1, cert 81043392):** graded and scanned green, voided red, scanned at the shop, checked at the end.
- **The printed list:** printed, highlighted, then its Enter key turns the city's screens red.

## Hero moments ★
- **S01 Rewind hook:** the strike, freeze and rewind. It's the scroll-stopper.
- **S04 The strike for real:** the hammer break you loved, now with the doors swinging open and the drag.
- **S12 The red scan:** the shop scanner beam crosses the label, the screen and the whole shop flash red, customers turn, and the clerk's eyes go to the thief. This is the climax.

**Route:** sky → A (down onto the truck) → A (the strike) → B (through a shipping label) → C (dissolve) → A (out the window to the police) → C (through the keyhole) → aerial (crane out of the skylight) → D (down into the shop window) → E (follow the thief out) → E night (dissolve) → B (dissolve) → back along the whole route → A at frame 0.

**2 hard cuts:** the FBI car's arrival and the shop reaction.

## Cast
- **Thief:** beanie and eye mask, carries the hammer. In S12 he adds a "disguise" over it: sunglasses and a stick-on moustache. That's the gag.
- **FedEx driver:** cap, clipboard, coffee cup; whistles as he walks off.
- **Collector:** cap and hoodie. Waits at the empty mailbox; later the careful buyer.
- **PSA grader and staffer:** lab coat / shirt and lanyard.
- **Police officer:** police hat, evidence bag.
- **FBI agent:** black suit, sunglasses, jacket lettered FBI.
- **Shop clerk:** holds the scanner.
- **Reaction chorus:** 2–3 shop customers who turn on the red flash.

Views needed: front and side for the thief, driver and collector; front for the rest.

## Visual laws
- **Cert number status:** the scanner beam plus a beep. **Green = on record, red = voided.** The voided label also glows faintly red. Used in S10 (green), S11 (rows flip red, the city's screens go red), S12 (red at the shop), S14 (red on the phone) and S15 (green at home).
- **Counting:** the fanfold list stacks up in its tray, filmed in one locked frame. Yellow highlighter marks the Pokémon rows, and nearly half the stack turns yellow.
- **Numbers and the date:** house titles (Bolota): NGÀY 13 THÁNG 10 · 6.451 MÓN HÀNG · ~3.000 POKÉMON · $1.000.000.

## Gags
This script has no spoken exaggerations, so the gags are visual and change no facts:
- the driver whistling off with his coffee;
- a fly buzzing out of the empty mailbox;
- the thief's sunglasses-and-moustache disguise over his eye mask;
- the FBI car screeching in;
- the thief's coat lined with slabs in the private sale.
