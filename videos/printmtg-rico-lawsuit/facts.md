# Wizards v. PrintMTG (RICO): facts
Sources:
- https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63961/wizards-coast-sues-magic-the-gathering-proxy-maker (24 Nov 2026)
- https://kotaku.com/wizards-of-the-coast-files-lawsuit-against-counterfeit-magic-the-gathering-card-printing-business-2001000110
- https://www.mtgsalvation.com/news/50147-wizards-of-the-coast-sues-proxy-printer-printmtg

**All of this is allegation in a civil complaint. Nothing has been decided by a court.**

## Confirmed
- **Filing:** 23 Nov 2026 (MTG Salvation), US District Court for the Western District of Washington (ICv2). The complaint runs 97 pages.
- **Defendants:** Ryan James Dunn and others (the Dunn family in Utah, including Melissa Dunn; Dunn-owned businesses; relatives; associates), collectively "PrintMTG Enterprise".
- **The business:**
  - sold printed Magic cards as "proxies", marketed as "as realistic as possible";
  - main site Proxy King (proxyking.biz, now down);
  - an Etsy storefront allegedly offered Pokémon proxies, and Kotaku says the suit also covers Pokémon cards and Lego minifigs.
- **How they look:** near-identical to real cards; the main visible difference is a purple colour pip on the back (MTG Salvation).
- **Claims:**
  - copyright infringement;
  - civil RICO (mail fraud, wire fraud, money laundering);
  - trademark counterfeiting and infringement;
  - Washington Consumer Protection Act.
- **Complaint quote (via ICv2):** "A counterfeit is made to be mistaken for a real card and to take its place as something owned, traded, or sold."
- **Damages:**
  - actual or treble damages, or statutory damages of up to $28.65 million "based on the initial infringing acts identified" (ICv2);
  - Kotaku says $28M and MTG Salvation over $26M;
  - the script says "có thể tới hai mươi tám triệu đô".
- **Other relief:** surrender of printing equipment, an injunction.
- **Response:** none from the defendants reported.
